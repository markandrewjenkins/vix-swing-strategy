About this dashboard

What this is

A dashboard depicting a VIX term-structure–oriented swing trading strategy, backtested and presented with a live signal monitor. It was built primarily with Claude Code, on top of the author’s own research into VIX term-structure behaviour and volatility ETFs developed over several years. The engine is Python; the dashboard is a single self-contained page, and the whole pipeline updates itself in the cloud.

Why it was built

This is a personal quantitative research project, intended for demonstration and educational purposes only: to show the strategy’s logic, its backtest, and how its signals are read day to day. It also exists to explore what modern AI coding tools — in particular Claude Code — can do when pointed at a real quantitative-finance problem end to end: the data pipeline, the signal engine, the backtest and this live front-end were all built collaboratively with AI. It is a personal project, not a product or a service. More of the author’s work can be found on his portfolio webpage.

How to read it

Live Chart & Signals — price with entry/exit markers plus the live signal monitor (hover or click any bar to see exactly what the strategy saw that day). P&L Summary — equity curve and performance stats. Trades Detail — every closed trade plus the current open position, with the rule that closed it and where the reading sat versus its threshold. Risk Analysis — Monte Carlo, rolling returns, MAE/MFE. Markets & Regime — risk/return versus buy-and-hold and performance by market regime. Forward-Looking Analysis — what happened after the most similar past setups, with the method’s own measured track record. The toggle at the top switches all figures between the SVXY/UVXY pair and the primary SVIX / UVIX strategy (SVIX⅞ base, scaled up to a full SVIX position on dips; UVIX at 86% of the account).

Data & updates

Quotes and the term structure come from public sources (CBOE end-of-day files, Yahoo intraday, FRED) and are delayed (~5–15 min). Everything refreshes automatically in the cloud — live quotes and candles roughly every five minutes through the session, and the historical signals after each close — so the page stays current without anything running locally.

Limitations

Data is provided as-is with no guarantee of accuracy, completeness or availability — feeds can lag or fail, and a failed refresh leaves the last good values on screen. SVIX/UVIX before their 2022 launch are simulated from SVXY/UVXY returns, leverage-scaled, so any figure spanning that period is partly modelled rather than traded (scaled by each fund’s leverage at the time — SVXY and UVXY changed leverage in Feb-2018). The backtest fills at the 4:00 PM close, while live orders would be placed in after-hours (~7–8 PM ET) where liquidity is thinner and prices can differ. Results do not include slippage, commissions, borrow costs or taxes, and dollar figures ignore capacity limits. The rule thresholds were developed on this same 2011–2026 history (with out-of-sample checks), so results are partly in-sample and are likely to overstate live performance. The strategy has not been traded live at scale.

About this strategy

The idea

This is a VIX term-structure swing strategy. It trades two volatility ETFs: SVXY (short volatility: −0.5× the VIX short-term futures index since Feb-2018, −1× before) and UVXY (long volatility: 1.5× since Feb-2018, 2× before). The toggle at the top switches to the primary SVIX / UVIX configuration (the same signals traded through the newer, more leveraged funds).

When it goes short vol (buys SVXY)

When the VIX curve is in contango (longer-dated VIX above near-dated) and the term-structure signals align bullish — contango, VDelta, the VIX9D calm zone and the 1M/3M ratio — plus a BB-trend confirmation. Contango means short vol is paid to hold (positive roll yield).

When it goes long vol (buys UVXY)

When the full four-signal bear regime is in place (acute near-term fear with the front of the curve inverted), or when a volume-divergence “vol-follow” setup appears with a bearish front end. A sudden one-day VIX collapse (“crush”) is also monitored as an override, though in the backtest it has never been the deciding trigger for a trade. A guard blocks fresh long-vol entries while VIX9D sits in an “exhaustion zone” — the level where an initial spike has tended to mean-revert in the backtest, so a fresh long there often loses.

How it exits

Six independent exit rules are checked each evening — take-profit, stop-loss, time stop, negative carry, MOVE/VIX cross-asset stress, and term-structure rollover. The moment any fires, the position is closed — with one exception: a short-vol exit from the last three is held while the short-vol entry conditions are still met that same evening, so the strategy never sells into a bar where its own logic would buy. Take-profit and the time stop always fire.

Timing & execution

Signals use the day’s close plus the CBOE end-of-day term structure (posts ~5:30 PM ET); the decision is made by ~7 PM ET and the trade executes the same day in after-hours (~7–8 PM ET). No intraday repaint — everything is decided on confirmed daily data.

Position sizing — dynamic

The primary SVIX / UVIX configuration holds the short-vol leg at SVIX⅞ (seven-eighths of the account; cut back in a high-vol regime) and puts 86% of the account into UVIX for long-vol trades. Sizing then adapts mid-trade: if an open short-vol position draws down past a set level (and the spike isn’t still building), the strategy adds to a full SVIX position (capped at 100% of the account — no margin) — adding after a drawdown that, in the backtest, has tended to recover; once the blended position recovers to its trim level it trims back to SVIX⅞, banking the add-on’s profit. These ADD/TRIM events are marked on the chart and listed in Trades Detail. The headline drawdown is driven by the short-vol “grind”, not the long-vol trades.
